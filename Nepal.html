History of Nepal

A brief journey through the major periods and turning points in the history of Nepal, from ancient kingdoms to the modern federal democratic republic.

Ancient and Early Medieval Period

The history of Nepal begins with small kingdoms and tribal confederations in the Himalayan region. The Kathmandu Valley, home to the Newar civilization, became an important center of trade and culture.

  • c. 7th–3rd century BCE – The region is mentioned in ancient Indian texts as the home of various tribes and small states.
  • c. 563 BCE – Siddhartha Gautama (Buddha) is born in Lumbini, in present-day southern Nepal.
  • c. 4th–9th century CE – The Licchavi dynasty rules much of the Kathmandu Valley, building many temples and monuments.

Malla Period

After the Licchavis, the Malla kings came to power in the Kathmandu Valley. This period is known for its rich art, architecture, and development of city-states.

  • c. 12th–18th century – The Malla dynasty rules, with three major rival kingdoms: Kathmandu, Patan, and Bhaktapur.
  • 14th–17th century – Many of the famous Durbar Squares and pagoda-style temples in the valley are built.

Unification of Nepal

Modern Nepal was formed when the small hill kingdom of Gorkha began to conquer neighboring states and unify them into a single kingdom.

  • 1743 – Prithvi Narayan Shah becomes king of Gorkha and starts a campaign to unify the many small kingdoms of the region.
  • 1768 – Kathmandu is captured; the foundations of a unified Kingdom of Nepal are laid.
  • Late 18th–early 19th century – Expansion continues, but clashes with the British East India Company eventually halt Nepalese advances.

Rana Rule and Foreign Relations

In the mid-19th century, the Rana family took control of Nepal, establishing a hereditary prime ministership and limiting the power of the monarchy. The country remained largely isolated.

  • 1846 – Jang Bahadur Rana seizes power after the Kot massacre and establishes the Rana regime.
  • 1814–1816 – Earlier, the Anglo-Nepalese War leads to the Treaty of Sugauli, defining much of Nepal’s modern borders.
  • 19th–early 20th century – Nepal maintains independence but becomes closely aligned with British India, sending Gurkha soldiers to fight in British wars.

Democracy, Monarchy, and Conflict

The 20th century saw movements for democracy, shifts in royal power, and internal conflict that reshaped Nepal’s political landscape.

  • 1951 – The Rana regime is overthrown with the support of pro-democracy forces and the monarchy is restored with a more open government.
  • 1960 – King Mahendra dissolves parliament and introduces the partyless Panchayat system under royal rule.
  • 1990 – A popular movement forces the king to accept constitutional monarchy and multi-party democracy.
  • 1996–2006 – A Maoist insurgency leads to a civil conflict, causing major political and social changes.

Republic of Nepal

After years of conflict and negotiation, Nepal transformed from a kingdom into a federal democratic republic.

  • 2001 – The royal massacre leads to widespread shock and political instability.
  • 2006 – The People’s Movement forces King Gyanendra to give up direct rule; peace agreements end the civil war.
  • 2008 – The monarchy is abolished and Nepal is declared a federal democratic republic.
  • 2015 – A new constitution is adopted, defining Nepal as a secular, federal republic with seven provinces.

Today, Nepal is working to strengthen its democratic institutions, rebuild after devastating earthquakes, and balance tradition with modernization.
